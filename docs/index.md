## Research statement

My research lies at the intersection of strategic leadership and corporate social evaluations. I study how external audiences judge and challenge firms, and how those pressures shape governance and strategic outcomes inside the organization. Although public disagreement over corporate behavior is not new, digital platforms have transformed how social evaluations form, evolve, change, and influence the firm. Management research has traditionally assumed that firm reputations can be represented through established mass media outlets and contested by powerful, high-salience stakeholders such as institutional investors or activists. On social media, however, narrative power becomes decentralized. Fragmented communities and general audiences, even those with little power and salience over a firm, can rapidly mobilize around shared opinions and identities to turn executive actions or misconduct into major corporate challenges.

In my current work, I examine the influence of stakeholder challenge on firm’s governance and strategic choice. One stream investigates [how broad stakeholder scrutiny shapes board attention during CEO succession](/projects/#ceo-succession), exploring when external pressures distract directors from selecting a leader who fits the firm’s strategic characteristics. Collaborative work similarly traces [how social activism alters the pace and direction of strategic decision-making](/projects/#social-activism). Together, these projects highlight a central premise of my research: external evaluations reach beyond just public image to potentially alter how firms are governed and led.

Building on this foundation, I am developing a research agenda focused on [how top managers’ actions and narratives interact with polarized online publics](/projects/#executive-narratives). Chief executives increasingly serve as the public face of their organizations. Some notable individuals publicly take partisan stance by endorsing political figure. Some lean on highly nationalistic narratives, framing corporate strategy around broad political initiatives. Because of the fragmented and polarized nature of online communities (and actual communities), executive rhetoric that builds legitimacy with one community can simultaneously alienate another. I hope to understand how audiences perceive and mobilize around executive and corporate narratives and actions. For example, when peripheral online groups gain disproportional voice to create real influence over firms, and whether leaders’ symbolic claims may lock companies into substantive strategic commitments by catering to certain audiences.

To study these dynamics, I aim to employ a broad spectrum of methodologies, including qualitative narrative and discourse approaches and computational methods. I take advantage of modern technologies to understand semantic in text and multimodal sources at scale. Ultimately, by connecting the socio-psychological realities of digital communication with strategic management, I seek to clarify how modern organizations navigate legitimacy, leadership, and strategy when consensus is elusive and almost any audience can express their voice.
